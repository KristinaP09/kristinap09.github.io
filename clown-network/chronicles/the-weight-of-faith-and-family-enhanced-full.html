The Weight of Faith and Family

Now With 200% More Sarcasm and Zero Surprise

Oh look, the words “friend,” “family,” and “community” have once again ghosted me — probably because even they have standards. Can’t blame them. Here they’re basically seasonal decorations: strung up for Instagram, screamed from pulpits, weaponized mid-conversation, then quietly tossed in the spiritual trash the second Monday hits.

Yesterday I refreshed my classic social experiment: “Has humanity patched this bug yet?” Spoiler: patch notes still say “working as intended.” My mother — Patron Saint of Mixed Signals — casually told a church lady who bought an umbrella, “asa tor sombuh” (“hope someone gets better soon”). I wasn’t even sick. But sure, throw the shade anyway. Blessings here come with free side of judgment. Passive-aggression isn’t a bug; it’s the native language, and the accent is impeccable.

Church? Peak performance art. Sunday is basically Righteousness Met Gala. Everyone struts in their best piety filter, recites verses like cheat codes, fake-concerns for 90 minutes, then speed-runs back to weekday villain arc. They call it fellowship. I call it group hallucination with better lighting and worse snacks.

I used to think basic decency was armor. No debt, no drama, no public meltdowns — just quiet, boring competence. Hilarious premise. Turns out decency just makes you premium-grade free labor. The crooked ones? They’re VIP. They monetize chaos. My siblings graduated top of the class in public applause / private sabotage. Their love comes with terms & conditions, restocking fees, and an aggressively short shelf life. So I upgraded to premium silence. Best decision of the decade.

Go ahead, slap the anak durhaka label on me — I’ll frame it. First adult truth I ever internalized: leave before they finish mining your soul for parts. Walking away from lifelong emotional malware isn’t cute. It’s major surgery without anesthesia. But staying? That’s just agreeing to be the family ATM with feelings.

And please — this isn’t “just my family.” It’s the national special edition. Patriarchy here isn’t a bug; it’s the constitution, preserved in amber and worshipped like fine wine that somehow tastes like regret. Batak, Javanese, Nias, doesn’t matter — same script: men awarded lifetime achievement trophy at birth, zero DLC required. Women? Auto-assigned to eternal unpaid overtime + guilt DLC. Call it adat, call it respect, call it whatever helps you sleep.

The male confidence is genuinely avant-garde. No skills, no empathy, no receipts — yet somehow they’re walking around like God took a personal day to hand-sculpt them and then retired satisfied. Meanwhile women have to submit a 400-page CV of suffering just to be acknowledged as human. Groundbreaking stuff.

And the cheer squad? Women who stan walking red flags like it’s a full-time job. They defend, romanticize, compete, excuse, reproduce. Garbage♂ + Garbage♀ = next season’s louder, emptier cast. Self-cleaning pipeline of mediocrity proudly sponsored by Tradition™. If mediocrity were an Olympic sport, we’d have more gold than anyone could count.

So yes — most of these “pillars of the family/community” are less useful than actual garbage. At least garbage gets recycled or warns you not to touch it. These guys just demand worship for breathing and collapse the second someone stops clapping. And the women propping up the theater? Not victims. Co-producers. Standing ovation for everyone involved in this tragicomedy.

Last night’s dinner special: same house, same script, new inconsistencies. Rental story changed faster than my brother’s excuses. Motorcycle price? Depends on the moon phase. Ruko plans? Schrödinger’s real estate. I chose violence: silence. It’s free, low-calorie, and surprisingly effective.

Said brother — the system’s poster child — brags about koneksi-funded 200 juta job while calling my degree useless. Sweetheart, I paid cash for that degree with actual teaching money while you were busy being “connected.” Taiwan master’s visa died because emotional/financial backup was apparently optional. PhD? Doing it solo. Independence is late, but at least it shows up.

After the lies, the theater, the diamond-cut cruelty — faith, friends, family, community? All expired brands of the same placebo. I’m out of stock on belief. Permanently.

I still wake up at 3 a.m. with the chest weight and brain fog. Maybe grief. Maybe just carbon monoxide from decades of breathing everyone else’s nonsense. Either way, until I taste actual freedom — not the Instagram version — I’ll keep doing what works: sarcasm as Kevlar, brutal honesty as the middle finger, and silence as the last polite way to say “I’m done.”
